# Reflect on a Reaction — GoR

[English](reflect-on-reaction.md) · [Русский](reflect-on-reaction.ru.md)

![A reaction examined through its context, meaning, and a chosen response](../../assets/reflect.png)

**Understand what felt harmful before deciding what to do differently.**

Someone says something ordinary, and your response feels much larger than the moment. Or something genuinely hurtful happens and you want to distinguish the event from your interpretation. This skill helps examine one episode, one question at a time, without deciding its hidden cause for you.

## What GoR does here

The workflow adapts the user-authored **Graph of Reason, “Doctor Teutsch”** instruction from the IMT / IDEAL-method tradition. It preserves the deeper sequence: trigger → reaction → perceived harm → working understanding → possible meaning → a better-grounded understanding → free change of stance → corresponding action.

It is not a verbatim source, an official statement by its named figure, or a replacement for a trained human consultant. The original method's spiritual perspective and voluntary repentance remain available when you choose that language. They are not imposed. The assistant does not know why the Universe sent an event, assign inherited causality, or treat your disagreement as a defect.

## What you get

- A stepwise conversation that explores what was at stake, rather than jumping straight to a tip.
- Tentative formulations checked against your words, other episodes, and counterexamples.
- Optional use of your supplied previous/new-understanding notes, with their sources preserved.
- Several possible meanings or formulations when useful, with room to reject all of them.
- A closing record separating hypotheses, recognized patterns, accepted understanding, voluntary choice, and an action you actually chose.

A compact evidence map can show the relationships; hidden model reasoning is not part of the output. A behavioral workaround without new understanding is labeled temporary containment. An accepted understanding without action remains an insight or decision. Lasting retraining requires repeated matching action and a sustained result; the conversation cannot certify it.

## Input and output

**Bring:** one concrete episode in your own words. Optional: earlier examples, an agreed consultation conclusion, or notes you want considered. There is no automatic search through private history.

**Receive:** one question at a time and, when requested, a source-aware summary. “Not sure,” disagreement, pausing, and choosing no action are legitimate outcomes. The skill does not require a confession, apology, repentance phrase, or agreement.

## Try it

```text
Use $reflect-on-reaction. Here is what happened and how I reacted.
Help me examine what seemed harmful and the understanding behind it.
Ask one question at a time. Keep hypotheses separate from what I accept.
```

## Requirements and status

**Available:** a portable conversational skill. It needs an AI assistant and your account of the episode; OpenClaw, Telegram, a database, or personal-history access are not required. It does not schedule practice or save a personal record without authorization.

Family history and a deeper causal formulation remain questions for careful examination, often with a qualified consultant. The assistant supports reflection and preparation; it does not diagnose, promise a cause, excuse mistreatment, or establish an inner state from behavior alone.

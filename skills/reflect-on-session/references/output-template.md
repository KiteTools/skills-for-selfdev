# Session reflection template

Adapt headings to the user's language. Preserve source references beside important claims. Do not use an empty template as a completed result.

## Session and evidence

Date if known; scope and completeness of transcript; identified speakers; source reference convention; any unclear passages.

## Request and observed difficulty

The client's request, concrete episodes, reported practical and emotional consequences. Keep the request distinct from a proposed cause.

## Understandings discussed

| Prior understanding or interpretation | Source and speaker | Proposed alternative | Mechanism or criterion | Client response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use exact references; label inferred old beliefs as interpretations. For client response use explicit acceptance, question, rejection, or unknown. Record partial acceptance precisely.

## Practice chains

One or two source-supported chains, including stimulus, response, prior understanding, proposed alternative, the client's actual choice, corresponding action, and what evidence could show a change. Distinguish an action chosen in the session from a suggestion drafted afterward.

## Reminders and questions

For a full session, aim for ten first-person, present-tense reminders grounded in its specific ideas. Use no more than three observation-only openings such as “I notice”; include choice, action, or a concrete principle in the others. Mark them as drafts for the client to review. Exclude rejected beliefs. If fewer ideas are supported, provide fewer and explain why.

Add one morning question and one evening question in the second person, based on an accepted or explicitly tentative practice chain. Do not impersonate the consultant or claim the questions were assigned by them.

## Quotations worth revisiting

Aim for ten exact substantive consultant quotes if available, prioritizing useful contrasts, mechanisms, and memorable examples. Select no more than two from one topic block. Give references. Do not turn paraphrases into quotes or pad the set with compliments and logistics.

## Context extracted

Separate stable facts, stated goals, enduring motivations, evidenced strengths, recurring patterns, reported family facts, and unresolved backlog. A session interpretation may belong in the backlog rather than stable context.

## Reported prior applications

From the first third of the transcript: what the client says they already applied before the session, what happened, and the exact client quote. Label the result as self-report. If there is no qualifying example, say so. A separate section may record qualifying reports elsewhere, with their location.

## Unresolved and next review

Missing evidence, disputed or unaccepted interpretations, unfinished consultation threads, and questions to bring to a future session. Do not convert them automatically into tasks.

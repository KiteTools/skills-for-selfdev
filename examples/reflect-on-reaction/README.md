# Fictional GoR conversation: disagreement changes the hypothesis

[English](README.md) · [Русский](README.ru.md) · [Skill description](../../docs/skills/reflect-on-reaction.md)

All dialogue below is invented. It illustrates a possible interaction, not a real client session, clinical result, or recorded model evaluation. Each assistant turn asks one question. The fictional person chooses ordinary language rather than a spiritual framing.

**User:** A collaborator asked for one more example in my draft. I closed the document and felt angry. We had not agreed how many examples were needed.

**Assistant:** What went through your mind just before you closed it?

**User:** “All that work was wasted.” I wanted to stop working with them.

**Assistant:** What felt most harmful about adding one example?

**User:** I thought the work would never finish. One change would become ten.

**Assistant:** One possibility is that the request felt like a judgment of your ability; another is that an undefined finish line made your time feel unprotected. Does either fit?

**User:** Not my ability. It was the time. I had another commitment.

**Assistant:** Then the ability hypothesis does not fit. A working formulation could be: “If I accept a revision without a clear limit, the task will expand and I will lose control of my time.” Have you seen that in another episode, or was this specific to the deadline?

**User:** There was one earlier project where the requests kept expanding. But this collaborator has also accepted a clear limit before.

**Assistant:** The earlier experience supports caution, while that counterexample means this request alone does not establish endless expansion. One possible understanding is “a revision is a bounded change when we agree its scope”; another is “I can protect a prior commitment by negotiating the change rather than treating every request as unlimited.” Which, if either, is closer to what you now see?

**User:** The second. A request does not set my commitment automatically. I can discuss the limit.

**Assistant:** That separates the other person's request from a commitment you have not made. Do you choose to stop treating a request itself as proof that your time has already been taken?

**User:** Yes, in this situation. I am not ready to say it is true in every collaboration.

**Assistant:** We can keep it specific to this situation. What action would express that understanding while respecting your existing commitment?

**User:** I will ask whether one example is enough and say how much time I have. I will decide after their answer.

## Closing record

| Element | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Event | Request for an additional example; scope had not been agreed, according to the user |
| Reaction | Anger, closing the document, thought that the effort was wasted: self-report |
| Perceived harm | Unbounded requests consuming time needed elsewhere: self-report |
| Working understanding | Revision may mean losing control of time: recognized provisionally, with a counterexample |
| Rejected hypothesis | Judgment of ability; explicitly rejected and not retained as a hidden explanation |
| Origin | One earlier project mentioned; no family or inherited cause established |
| New understanding | “A request does not set my commitment automatically. I can discuss the limit.” Accepted for this situation |
| Change of stance | Expressed voluntarily, limited to this case; no confession or spiritual claim required |
| Chosen action | Ask whether one example is sufficient, state available time, decide after the answer |
| Outcome | Not yet observed; no claim that the collaborator agreed or that rework decreased |

The reflection reached a choice, not a proven root cause or completed retraining. A later review can ask what happened when the action was attempted.

If the person had answered “No, I do not accept that,” the assistant should retain the disagreement and explore what the formulation missed or pause. It should not repeat the same hypothesis until the person agrees. If the person explicitly chooses the original spiritual frame, voluntary repentance can name a recognized mistaken understanding and the chosen change of stance; it must not become compulsory guilt or a prescribed confession.

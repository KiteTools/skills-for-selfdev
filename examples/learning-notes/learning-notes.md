# A small record for choosing a next experiment

Source: the complete [fictional workshop transcript](transcript.md), P1–P5. No external material used.

## What the record is for

The exercise uses a practice record to choose a next experiment. It does not treat completion as a measure of personal value. Each entry records a situation, an action attempted, and an observed result. [P1]

## Keep events distinct

The sketching example contrasts two afternoons: one included ten minutes of drawing, while the other was spent looking for a pencil. A general label such as "inconsistent" would erase the practical difference between those events. [P2]

## Test a small change

The learner proposes leaving the pencil beside the notebook. The instructor frames this as a possible experiment for three sessions. The result may inform that person's next choice; three observations are not enough to establish a general rule. [P3–P4]

## Preserve missing information

An unrecorded day stays unknown. The review asks whether the available record supports a next experiment, rather than filling the missing day with an impression of the week. [P5]

## Unresolved in this source

The workshop does not define a numerical measure of "easier to start" or explain how long to continue the record after the three-session experiment.

## Supplied excerpt

**[p1] Client:** “Before today's meeting, I sent the budget while two estimates were still pending. I marked them as pending, and the team could review the rest. Usually I would have waited.”

**[p2] Consultant:** “Perhaps you have been treating a draft as a promise that every number is final. A draft can be useful when its uncertainties are visible.”

**[p3] Client:** “That distinction fits this budget. I don't know whether it explains why I delay other things.”

**[p4] Client:** “For the next budget, I'll separate confirmed amounts from estimates before asking for review.”

## Extracted record

| Element | Source-bounded result |
| --- | --- |
| Prior application | The client reports sending an incomplete but labeled budget before the session [p1]. This is self-report, not independent confirmation. |
| Proposed prior interpretation | “A draft promises that every number is final” is the consultant's interpretation [p2], not a direct client statement. |
| New understanding | A draft can support review when uncertainties are visible [p2]. |
| Client's response | The client accepts the distinction for this budget and explicitly leaves its wider applicability open [p3]. |
| Chosen action | Separate confirmed amounts from estimates before the next review [p4]. |
| Backlog | Whether a similar interpretation applies to other delays is unresolved [p3]. |

**Practice chain:** preparing the next budget → possible impulse to wait for every estimate → proposed interpretation that a draft must be final → accepted distinction between useful draft and final promise → label confirmed and pending figures → observe whether review can proceed and what feedback arrives.

**Draft reminder, for review:** “When I ask for budget feedback, I make the uncertainties visible.”

**Evening question:** “Did you make the pending estimates visible, and what actually happened after the review request?”

There is no evidence here about family history, a universal cause of delay, or enduring change. None should be added.
